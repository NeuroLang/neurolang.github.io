Calibrating the similarity gate of similar_study#

The meta-analysis engines (neurosynth, neurostore) define the studies about a free text, similar_study(q, s), from the SPECTER2 similarity of the text to each study’s title and abstract (Semantic Search Over Study Abstracts). This page explains how the rule is built, why it is built this way, and how its threshold is calibrated for an engine or an embedding model.

What similar_study does#

The rule has two steps, both computed once per text by query_similarity_cutoff(q):

similar_study(q, s) :- study(s) & (v == query_similarity(q, s)) & (c == query_similarity_cutoff(q)) & (v >= c)

  1. A gate on the text. The mean similarity of the text’s 20 closest studies (the top-20 mean) must reach the similarity_gate of the engine’s text_embeddings entry (Text embeddings). If it does not, no study is about the text: the cutoff is inf and similar_study is empty.

  2. A cutoff on the studies. If the gate passes, the cutoff is the 95th percentile of the text’s positive similarities: the text gets the top 5% of the studies closest to it.

A text that passes the gate gets its whole top 5%, a text that fails it gets nothing.

Why not a floor, or standardised similarities#

Every text has a top 5%, even one that no study is about: ‘stock market crash’ would get 719 studies of NeuroSynth. A floor on each study’s similarity, v >= 0.75, does not fix this well. SPECTER2 similarities have a baseline that depends on the text: over NeuroSynth, the median similarity of a text to the studies ranges from 0.57 to 0.76. So a floor measures how much the wording of a text looks like neuroscience more than how many studies are about it. Within the top 5%, it keeps a fragment decided by a few outlying studies: 5 studies for ‘tapping fingers in sequence’, 32 for ‘imagining future events’, and all 719 for the Cognitive Atlas definition of ‘memory’.

Standardising the similarities of each text, as z = (v - median) / MAD or (v - mean) / sd, does not tell the two kinds of texts apart either. A text off the domain also has a few studies standing out from its own distribution, so the largest z, the mean z of the 20 closest studies and the number of studies above z >= k have a ROC AUC of 0.44 to 0.65 against texts off the domain. The calibration procedure#

To calibrate the gate for an engine or an embedding model:

  1. A null set. Write a few hundred texts that no study is about, of varied topics (cooking, sport, finance, history, law…) and lengths, from short phrases to definition-length paragraphs.

  2. An in-domain set. Take the definitions of the Cognitive Atlas (cogat_definition(t, d)), the topics users ask about, and short task phrases such as ‘tapping fingers in sequence’.

  3. A fit/check split. Split each set into two halves at random: a fit half, to choose the statistic and its threshold, and a check half, to measure them.

  4. The statistics. Score every study against each text with scores(), and compute the candidate statistics of each text: the largest score, the mean of the 20 largest, the median, and their standardised versions.

  5. ROC AUC. On the fit half, compare the in-domain texts with the null by the ROC AUC of each statistic, and keep the simplest statistic with a high AUC.

  6. The threshold. Set it at a high percentile of the statistic over the null texts of the fit half. Its false-positive rate is about one minus that percentile: 10% at the 90th, 5% at the 95th, 1% at the 99th.

  7. The check. On the check half, measure the share of null texts that pass (the false-positive rate) and the share of in-domain texts kept.

  8. The setting. Set the threshold as similarity_gate in the engine’s text_embeddings entry of engines.yaml. An engine that extends coordinate_meta_analysis inherits its 0.75 and overrides it in its own entry, as NeuroStore does:

    neurostore:
  extends: coordinate_meta_analysis
  text_embeddings:
    study_abstracts: {similarity_gate: 0.76}

In Python, for one text:

from pathlib import Path

import numpy as np
from neurolang_apps.utils.engine_registry import build_engine

nl = build_engine("neurosynth", Path("neurolang_data"))
index = nl.text_embedding_indexes["study_abstracts"]
scores = index.scores("medieval castle architecture")
print(np.sort(scores[scores > 0])[-20:].mean())   # 0.730: below the gate

Running the calibration script#

The script scripts/similarity_gate.py of neurolang-text2query-bench (branch neurosynth-v2) runs these steps:

python scripts/similarity_gate.py --engine neurostore \
    --data-dir neurolang_data \
    --cache runs/similarity_gate_scores_neurostore.npz \
    > runs/similarity_gate_report_neurostore.md

Its texts are in scripts/similarity_gate_texts/: null.txt, 328 texts off the domain, and domain_phrases.txt, 94 task phrases. The script adds the 1138 Cognitive Atlas definitions of the engine and the 27 topics and definitions used by the benchmark’s similar_study questions. --seed (0) draws the fit/check split, and --gate (0.75) is a threshold whose check-half rates the report gives. --cache keeps the scores of every text against every study, so that a rerun only recomputes the statistics; it is rebuilt when the texts change.

The report, in Markdown, has the AUC and the 95th and 99th percentile thresholds of every statistic with their check-half rates, the statistics of the short phrases, the benchmark topics and the null texts closest to the domain, the top-20 mean gate at the null’s 90th, 95th and 99th percentiles, and the AUC by text length. The reports of the two engines are runs/similarity_gate_report.md (NeuroSynth) and runs/similarity_gate_report_neurostore.md.

The gates are the 95th percentiles, rounded: 0.75 for NeuroSynth (2.4% of the check half’s null passes, 63% of its in-domain texts are kept) and 0.76 for NeuroStore (3.7%, 62%: 59% of the definitions, 71% of the benchmark topics, 87% of the task phrases), set in its own text_embeddings entry. NeuroStore needs a higher gate: with 4.5 times as many studies, the 20 closest to any text are closer, and 0.75 would let 9.1% of the check half’s null through. The statistic also separates the texts a little less well (AUC 0.905 against 0.934). The 99th percentile drops more in-domain texts on both engines, among them the benchmark topics ‘disgust at rotten food’ and ‘gambling with risky odds’ (top-20 means 0.751 and 0.755 on NeuroSynth, 0.765 and 0.763 on NeuroStore). A text that passes gets its whole top 5%: 719 studies of NeuroSynth, 3208 of NeuroStore.

Short texts are the hardest. On NeuroSynth, ‘tasting bitter drinks’ (top-20 mean 0.730) and ‘tapping fingers in sequence’ (0.746) get no study, ‘socially excluded’ (0.763) gets its top 5%. On NeuroStore the three are higher, 0.740, 0.757 and 0.777, and so are the gate’s outcomes: the first two fail 0.76, the third passes. A fuller description of the task, or the definition of a Cognitive Atlas concept, does better. A few texts outside the domain pass the gate, the highest being ‘golf handicap calculation’ (0.772 on NeuroSynth, 0.783 on NeuroStore).

Some studies are close to many texts: one is in the top 5% of 69% of the in-domain texts on NeuroSynth, of 77% on NeuroStore. Subtracting each study’s mean similarity to the null texts improves the AUC only a little (on the check half 0.955 against 0.939 on NeuroSynth, 0.941 against 0.912 on NeuroStore), so the gate does not correct for it.

Recalibrating for another encoder or engine#

The gate is a similarity, so it belongs to an encoder and a corpus: a different embedding model (e.g. a Qwen embedding served by the Albert API) has another scale of cosine similarities, and a different corpus moves the closest studies. To recalibrate:

  1. Make the engine’s index use the encoder: any object with the interface of Specter2Encoder (encode_documents, encode_query, cache_key) behind an EmbeddingIndex.

  2. Run the script on the engine with a new --cache: it only needs index.scores. Keep the same texts and seed, so that the results compare with the ones above.

  3. Check that the top-20 mean still has the best AUC, read its thresholds at the null’s 90th, 95th and 99th percentiles, and choose one by its check-half false-positive rate and in-domain share kept.

  4. Set similarity_gate in the engine’s text_embeddings entry, and note the numbers on this page.

A new engine over the same encoder needs the same: the null and the in-domain sets do not change, the studies do.
