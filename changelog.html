Changelog#

All notable changes to NeuroLang are documented here.

NeuroLang is in alpha. Pre-release versions (0.1aN) are published on PyPI; the Unreleased section below lists the changes on the current master branch that are not yet part of a numbered release.

Contributors: when adding a change, add an entry under Unreleased using the format below, then move it to the appropriate version section when releasing.

Unreleased#

New features:

  • Ontologies: the property axioms subPropertyOf, equivalentProperty, inverseOf, domain, range, SymmetricProperty and TransitiveProperty are read as constraints without existential variables, applied by the chase. A domain or range constraint, whose body has a variable out of its head, is chased with its rewritings, so Manager('Juan') holds when Juan heads an unnamed department of a headOf whose domain is Manager. Transitivity is never a rewriting step; its answers through unnamed values are outside the completeness guarantee of Ontologies. A predicate only in the bodies of constraints, with no facts or rules, answers the empty relation.

  • The query assistant names the columns of its answers after what they hold, ans(region, probability) rather than ans(l, p), and gives the entities an answer is computed over that the question names (the regions at the ends of a tract, the parcels of a network) as answers of their own, named queries ans[name](...): its syntax reference and ReAct steps say so, and the examples of the banks are rewritten so (a test checks their column names). New example on neurosynth_iit: the Bayes factor of the cognitive tasks mentioned in the studies activating the regions at both ends of the left uncinate fasciculus, with these regions.

  • Ontologies: the constraints without existential variables (subclass, allValuesFrom, hasValue, annotations) are applied by the chase, as Datalog rules; XRewrite rewrites the query with the existential ones, the others only on atoms that can stand for a value they introduce. A query on the Cognitive Atlas with an instance of a class high in its hierarchy now ends (ans(x) :- ro:has_part(x, y): 1.7 s, was > 120 s). New page Ontologies. Fixes on the way: XRewrite’s factorisation dropped the rest of the body, a rewriting step bound a query variable only in the rewritten atom (wrong answers with hasValue), a renamed constraint variable could take a query variable’s name, a negated atom made XRewrite fail, the chased predicates broke solve_all, an aggregate over a rewritten body counted per rewriting, and C ⊑ B ⊓ ∃p.D is read as C(x) → B(x).

  • neurolang-repl-serve --local loads the text embeddings of an engine when it builds it (its documents’, from their cache or computed into it, and the SPECTER2 query model): with --users in the builder, before forking the sessions, which share them; their first query_similarity (similar_study, lift_llm) no longer loads the model, and no longer crashes a session on macOS. --no-preload-embeddings (preload_embeddings: false) leaves them to the first query, as before. Queries are embedded with the numpy SPECTER2 encoder whenever its weights are next to the model, not only without torch; encoders of an engine built again share torch’s model.

  • The network of the brain viewer names what is under the mouse on a slice: a node, the name of its region; an edge, the names of the two regions it joins and its value (within hover_mm of viewer.yaml, 2 mm, of the sphere or tube drawn).

  • neurolang-repl-serve --activity-db gives the admins an activity page, /admin/activity: filters of the period, user and engine above figures (uptime, users, inputs and failures, CPU time, LLM tokens, the most memory of a session), a strip of the runs of the server, charts of the inputs, CPU, tokens and active users over time (Chart.js, served by the server), the users, and the inputs, sortable, searchable and each opening on its result. Datasette (--dashboard) stays, as Raw data and SQL. Its queries of the last days no longer take the whole first day (they compared ISO times with SQLite’s), and its uptime counts from the first run logged.

  • The engines neurostore_tracts and neurostore_iit: NeuroStore with tractography, as neurosynth_tracts and neurosynth_iit are Neurosynth with it (the vocabulary of tracts; the IIT whole-brain tractogram and tract_querier’s bundles). The page of the REPL offers neurostore_iit as NeuroStore + tracts, with the text embeddings of neurostore. What they share is two abstract engines, meta_analysis_tracts and iit_tractogram.

  • The login page of neurolang-repl-serve --users is the REPL’s: its logo and colours, in the theme the visitor chose in the REPL (or the system’s), a card with the name and the password, which can be shown, a button that waits while the server answers, the error above the form, Sign in with ORCID and Continue as a guest under or, and the request for an account as a card of its own. It is still one file, with the logo in it: the server refuses every other file before a login.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --local prepares each engine before serving (--no-prepare not to): it builds it once, in a process of its own, its downloads and its tables kept in --data-dir, so that a session starts from them without the network; one it cannot prepare is told, and the server serves the others. The bundle of neurolang-repl-serve keeps what its engines download in --downloads (~/.cache/neurolang/ repl-downloads), so that a rebuild does not need its sources (OSF, GitHub) to be up.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --config server.yaml: the server’s options in a YAML file, each by its name, its paths relative to it, the command line replacing them; the service installed with it runs the file. Its secrets (the ORCID client, the server’s env, the password, the key_password of the certificate’s key) in a second YAML file, secrets: secrets.yaml or --secrets, which the server refuses if others may read it.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --users users.yaml --orcid orcid.yaml: the users named by an ORCID iD sign in with ORCID (OAuth 2.0, scope /authenticate), with no password, their ORCID name shown; an iD of no user asks for an account, with a message, which an admin approves in the console (Account requests, adding them to groups) or with --approve-request ID --groups G, or declines.

  • The admin console of neurolang-repl-serve merges an ORCID user into a user of a name of their own (merge into…, --merge-orcid ID USER), who then logs in with their password or with ORCID (their orcid), in the groups of both, and separates them again (Separate ORCID, --separate-orcid USER): the iD a user of its own, in the groups but admin.

  • On their account page, a user of neurolang-repl-serve links their ORCID iD (Link my ORCID iD, signing in at ORCID; the account of the iD, if any, merged into theirs) and unlinks it; a user who signs in with ORCID only merges their account into one of theirs with a password, giving its name and password.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --local --users tracks the CPU seconds and the memory of each session’s process, its own apart from the engine’s data it shares with the builder (on Linux), shown in the admin console; and, with --activity-db FILE, logs what each user runs, its result as text, how long it took and the resources it took, in SQLite, with the totals of each user and their log in the console. The page tells its users their inputs are logged.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --local --users runs at most --max-running queries at once (the machine’s CPUs), of all the sessions, the others waiting their turn, their page saying so; and stops a query running longer than --query-minutes (60), ending its session’s process.

  • The REPL page of neurolang-repl-serve --local --users brings back a session the server ended (unused, made room for another, its query ran too long) at its next input: it starts it again, runs again the rules of the session, as after Stop, then that input. The server answers an ended session with 409, {"ended": true}.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --dashboard serves the admins a dashboard of --activity-db and the ratings at /admin/logs/: Datasette, with datasette-vega’s charts and queries of the activity by hour, user and engine, the slowest and failed inputs, the ends of the sessions and the uptime (the dashboard extra). GET /health tells a monitor whether the server serves, without a login; --daemon status what the service manager says of it; and the activity log has each run of the server, to its stop (a SIGTERM, as from systemd, now stops the server as Ctrl+C does, ending its sessions).

  • With --activity-db, an answer of the assistant logs the tokens it sent and received, its provider and model, and whose key paid (the page’s, or the server’s), shown by user in the console and by user, model and key in the dashboard. The REPL’s reply carries them (tokens).

  • The REPL in the browser is redesigned: the question and the query on the left, the answer on the right (one under the other on a narrow screen, which scrolls to the answer), in the colours of the logo and of the documentation, light and dark. A first visit finds an example in the box; the result pane says what comes there until a query runs; probabilities are drawn as tracks filled to their value, the most probable in red; the session commands are in a More menu.

  • The tables of the browser REPL have a Plot button: bars, a histogram, points or a line of their columns, of the rows the filter keeps.

  • A long rule the browser REPL puts in its box (an example, the assistant’s query, a follow-up) is laid out for the box’s width: its head on the left and an atom of its body a line, after :-; a long probability breaks before its //. More › Format the query (Alt+Shift+F) does it to what is typed.

  • The graph of a query (.graph, the Graph tab) opens fitted to the page, its whole in view, and zooms (−, Fit, +, Actual size, or Ctrl or Cmd and the wheel). A relation of an ontology is drawn as a node of a knowledge graph, linked to others; the name of a table is below the rim of its cylinder.

  • .view of pairs of regions or points with several value columns (ans(a, b, p, q)) draws the network of the first, the probability first, rather than every pair at 1; the viewer’s Edge value chooses the column the edges are of, its edge threshold following.

  • The brain viewer shows a range of values: a low and a high threshold (values or percentiles), with two sliders over a histogram of the values, the range shown in the colours it is drawn with.

  • In the browser REPL, a result has a Brain tab beside Table and Graph: it shows the result in the brain viewer.

  • The assistant takes at most 16 steps by default (max_steps, was 8), for questions that need more tool calls before a query that runs. The defaults of the LLM configuration are in one file, utils/llm/defaults.yaml, which the configuration reads; the example configuration and the docs quote it, and the tests check that they agree. So are the other tunable defaults: those of the brain viewer and .view (utils/tui/viewer.yaml), the options of neurolang-repl-serve (tools/serve_repl/defaults.yaml) and the limits of the browser REPL’s page (doc/_static/repl/settings.yaml, which the page reads at start).

  • When the assistant of the browser REPL finds no query, a notice under the question says so, why (the steps it used up, its last query’s error, or in its words what it found), that the query box still holds the query of before, and what to try: edit the question, edit its last query, see its steps, browse the examples.

  • The browser REPL keeps the questions asked in English, each with the query the assistant gave, between visits (Past questions, under the box of the assistant): Use this query puts it back in the box, in its language, with what the assistant said of it and the question it answers; Ask again puts the question back.

  • The query assistant answers a question naming a cognitive process, task or concept (“studies about working memory”, “the n-back task”) without saying mention, term or word with the studies closest to the concept’s Cognitive Atlas definition (cogat_definition with similar_study), looked up with search_values over cogat_term, or to the question’s own words when the atlas does not have it; mentions is kept for the questions about mentions, terms or words. The guidance and the examples of the coordinate_meta_analysis engines say so.

Performance:

  • The query assistant caches its prompts on the Anthropic API, and on OpenRouter for Claude: the steps of an answer repeat the system prompt and the conversation so far, which are then read from the cache at a tenth of the price of input tokens or less, making an answer about three times cheaper. The line after the query counts the tokens read from the cache, also for the providers that cache by themselves, such as DeepSeek on OpenRouter.

  • A natural join on a unique key of strings, such as the NeuroStore study ids joined with the probabilities of selected_study, looks the keys up by their codes: uniformity('pain', i, j, k, u) on neurostore takes 23 s instead of 81 s (2 mm grid).

  • A function of a rule’s variables, such as query_similarity(q, s) over the 65,000 studies of NeuroStore, is given each row as a tuple read by column, not as a pandas row: uniformity_llm('auditory stimuli', i, j, k, u) on neurostore spends 1 s instead of 15 s on it.

Bug fixes:

  • The semi-naive chase missed tuples in the first iteration of a recursive stratum: a rule’s recursive atom saw only the tuples found earlier in that iteration, not those of earlier strata. With rr(y, x) :- rr(x, y), p(x, y) :- rr(x, y) and rr(x, y) :- p(x, y), p lacked the first tuple of rr. In the first iteration a recursive atom now sees its whole relation.

  • An engine extending another with the same text_embeddings entry (neurosynth_iit, neurostore_iit, the _tracts ones) shares its caches (document embeddings, PubMed texts, model copies) in the embeddings directory of the most basic such engine (engine_registry.embeddings_owner), instead of embedding all the abstracts again under its own name, which took hours on NeuroStore. An engine whose documents differ from the shared embeddings, or whose own directory alone has them, keeps its own.

  • Arithmetic in the head of a rule (ans(x, n / 2) :- r(x, n)) was taken for an aggregation, the operator applied to each group’s values, and gave rows of missing values or failed in pandas; it is now refused with a ForbiddenExpressionError that tells how to compute the value in the body, (v == n / 2).

  • The threshold sliders of the brain viewer sit over the values they set on the histogram. In percentile mode a thumb was placed at its percentile along the slider while the histogram’s axis is the value, so for skewed maps the low thumb could be far from its line (128 px of 360 at the 50th percentile of cubed values); the thumbs are now placed by value, and the histogram is inset by half a thumb so its ends meet the thumbs’ travel.

  • Checking or running a query on an ontology’s predicates, as ans(t) :- cogat_term(c, 'working memory'), ro:has_part(c, x), cogat_term(x, t) on the neurosynth engine, takes seconds: stratifying the ontology’s 6.5k rules, one recursive component of 3364 predicates, ran for more than 30 minutes.

  • .view of streamlines with the regions they join, as ans(s, l1, l2) :- connects(s, l1, l2), draws the streamlines, their column of the sort streamline: it drew the network of the regions. The engine tracts declares the relations of a tractogram (endpoint_point, streamline_label…), so that their streamline is of that sort.

  • A disjunction of functions whose variables a positive atom outside it binds, as in ans(l) :- tian(l, r), (startswith('aHIP', l) | startswith('pHIP', l)), runs: it failed with UnboundVariablesError.

  • A probabilistic rule over another whose head repeats a variable, as b(c, s) :- a(c, s), roi(s) with a(s, s) :- term(s), selected_study(s), keeps all its body: the rule’s variables were not renamed apart from the query’s when its head was unified, and roi(s) was dropped (the probability, and the replicates of RESAMPLE, were those of term alone).

Bug fixes:

  • The query assistant’s guidance on the meta-analysis engines: the regions not activated by the studies about a topic get their probability of not being activated given the topic (~destrieux_activated over selected_study), not the regions no such study activates; and MNI coordinates are never the voxel indices of activated, lift or brain_mask.

Bug fixes:

  • The query assistant (ReAct) stops search loops: after two search_values calls finding no value it had not seen (a repeated call, a variant of a pattern), the observation asks for a query with the values found, run with run_query.

Bug fixes:

  • The memory limit of a query the assistant runs (query_memory_mb, 4 GB) counts the memory private to the forked process running it, not the engine’s relations it reads, which it shares with its parent: on Linux the resident memory of the child is the parent’s from the fork, so every query of an engine larger than the limit was stopped as using too much memory; on macOS a query reading activated (346 M rows on the 1 mm grid with the 6 mm kernel) was.

Bug fixes:

  • The assistant’s search_values searches a predicate whose columns have the same name, as cogat_subclass_of(iri, iri): it failed with 'DataFrame' object has no attribute 'str'.

Performance:

  • A Python function computing a column of a rule, as query_similarity, runs once per distinct row (pandas and polars backends): it ran on the duplicates of its input, 2 M rows instead of 14 k in cogat_definition('memory', d), similar_study(d, s), study_journal(s, 'Neuron'), which took 70 s instead of 3 s.

v0.1a22 (2026-11-13)#

Performance:

  • A Euclidean distance bound between a set of voxels and a set of peaks holds half the memory and takes about half the time: on 300 k peaks and the 318 k voxels of a 2 mm brain mask, a radius of 6 voxels (120 M pairs) peaks at 7.5 GB instead of 14.6 GB, and takes 18 s instead of 43 s, and a radius of 3 at 1.1 GB instead of 2.8 GB, in 1.6 s instead of 5.5 s. An integer join looked its neighbours up with the integer offsets of the radius only when it needed few look-ups, and built k-d trees otherwise, whose pairs are 5 to 10 times dearer than a look-up: it now looks them up whenever the larger set fills at least one cell of the grid of its coordinates in 40. The k-d trees are searched in chunks of the smaller set, so that the pairs they return are never all alive at once; and the distances, the 64-bit indices of the points and a copy of the pairs are no longer kept when they are not needed. Float coordinates lying on a grid (multiples of 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.1, 0.05 or 0.01, as the millimetre coordinates of peaks and voxels are) are joined the same way, as integers, the pairs at the very radius being checked on the floats: they give the same pairs and distances as before. The thresholds, the sizes of the chunks and the other tunable values of the join are in neurolang/utils/euclidean_join.yaml, read with OmegaConf (a dependency of neurolang now) and changed by the euclidean_join key of an engine (see Memory and time of Euclidean distance bounds), with a memory profile, which the interactive REPL, in a terminal and in a browser, uses: it uses the stencil up to a billion look-ups, counts the pairs before storing them and searches the k-d trees in small chunks. The distinct pairs are decoded in blocks into 32-bit indices: a radius of 6 voxels peaks at 6.6 GB, and at 6.0 GB in the memory profile, instead of 14.6 GB.

  • query_similarity(q, s) keeps the embeddings of the last 4,096 query texts and computes one dot product per call: a rule over many texts, cogat_definition(t, d), similar_study(d, s) over the 615 definitions of the Cognitive Atlas concepts, embedded a text again for nearly every row, as the rows come in any order and only the similarities of the last 16 texts were kept.

  • A rule with many negated atoms compiles in a time linear in them: each negation was joined with the difference of those before it, which put that difference in both operands and doubled the relational algebra with each negation (14 negations, as in the bundles of tract_querier translated to the vocabulary of the engine tracts, took most of an hour; now a second).

  • The regions of a large atlas are found in a second rather than ten (1.5 s instead of 12 s for the 180 labels of FreeSurfer’s 256³ wmparc): the labels are searched for in the type of the image’s values, all at once.

  • A selection by a Python function of a single column, as startswith('17Networks LH SalVentAttn', l) on schaefer_activated (291,533 rows) in a probabilistic rule, applies the function to the column instead of to every row: the probability of the Destrieux regions given a study activating a salience parcel of one hemisphere takes 2.4 s instead of 8.6 s, and of either hemisphere (a union of two rules) 12 s instead of 37 s.

  • The deterministic rest of a query that the disjoint separator joins with a probabilistic choice, as sub(t), mentions(s, t) in term_study(s) :- sub(t), mentions(s, t), selected_study(s), is computed as a set with the relational algebra, a union with a union and a projection, instead of with the operations on probabilities (sums, and a union of independent events through logarithms). The relational algebra optimiser flattens a projection of nested joins before pushing the projection into them, so that the joins are ordered by size; it joined the large relations first. The voxels activated by the studies mentioning a subclass of a Cognitive Atlas concept take 4.5 s instead of 6.2 s, and the Destrieux regions given a study activating a salience parcel of either hemisphere (a union of two rules with startswith) 15 s instead of 37 s, and 2.3 s with the column-wise selection of #1126. --show-ra draws these relations as trees.

  • Magic sets adorn first the body atoms holding a variable bound in the head, as they do the atoms with a constant: a query constant for a head variable, such as the text of p_a_given_similar('being socially excluded', i, j, k, p), restricts activated to the studies similar to it, as when the text is written into the rule, instead of joining the 50 M activations of every study in the probabilistic plan: 0.9 GB more than the engine instead of 4.2 GB, and 23 s instead of 45 s, for the same 1,151,590 rows.

  • RESAMPLE sums the probabilities of the outcomes of the choice over all the replicates at once, as a product of matrices, instead of joining every replicate with every outcome: the 500 replicates of the probability that two Schaefer parcels are active given a term (PROB[co_active(l1, l2) // term_association(s, 'auditory')]) take 13 s and 1 GB more than the engine, instead of 43 GB, and run in the REPL in the browser.

  • uniformity of a term (uniformity('pain', i, j, k, u)) takes 19 s instead of 55 s, and the memory of the engine instead of 18 GB, so that it runs in the REPL in the browser with the full engine (?engine=full): the chase projects a rule’s substitutions on the variables of its head, which the optimisers push into the joins, instead of computing, and comparing with the rows already found, all the combinations of the body’s variables (45 M rows of activations for 11 k of the head). A relation made from a data frame shares its columns, with copy-on-write, rather than copying them in one block, 1.4 GB for the activations.

  • lift of a term (lift('pain', i, j, k, l)) runs in the REPL in the browser with the full engine too: a look-up join (the probabilities of a choice) and a grouped sum, maximum, minimum or count of many rows go a chunk of 4 M rows at a time, which for the probability of each voxel over 45 M activations takes 0.5 GB instead of 1.2 GB and 0.2 GB instead of 1.9 GB, for a second more.

New features:

  • The meta-analysis engines (neurosynth, neurostore and those extending them) have the year, journal and abstract of each study, study_year, study_journal and study_abstract, next to study_title, and its identifiers, study_pmid and study_doi, the same in both engines, so that their studies are matched: Neurosynth’s abstracts are PubMed’s, NeuroStore’s years, PubMed ids and DOIs those it lists for its studies, asked for at the first build and kept in the data directory. A query, and the query assistant, restricts the studies to years or journals. The SPECTER2 similarity of neurosynth embeds its study_abstract, as neurostore does. See The publications of the studies in the engine configuration documentation.

  • The rest of the documentation starts from the REPL as well: the queries of the Cognitive Atlas page run on the neurostore engine from their link, with its counts in the 2026-11 release; Concepts writes facts and rules as the REPL takes them; the pages start the REPL as neurolang-query -i; the README leads with the REPL in the browser.

  • The SQUALL tutorial runs in the REPL too: its Bayes factor program is on the neurosynth engine and the Destrieux atlas, with the top 20 terms it gives, its aggregation examples on facts typed in the session and with the results they give, and its Python moves to NeuroLang in Python, where it is tested. SQUALL has no # comments: the examples that had them use % lines.

  • The Datalog tutorial runs in the REPL: its Bayes factor program is on the neurosynth engine and the Destrieux atlas, with the results and confidence intervals it gives, and its Python moves to a new page, NeuroLang in Python (NeuroLang in Python), which also takes NeuroLang for Logic Programmers (its address now leads there). The page’s examples are run by the tests.

  • The documentation starts from the REPL: a page on the REPL, in the browser and in a terminal (Using the REPL), a Quick Start and a Get Started whose examples are queries of the neurosynth engine, and, under each example of a page, a link that opens the REPL in the browser with it in its box (.. nl-repl::). The examples are coloured as the REPL colours them.

  • .view of a result with a value on the regions of an atlas (ans(l, p) :- PROB[...]) starts at the top 5% of its values, as a result of voxels does; regions alone, networks and streamlines start with all of them.

  • The examples of the parcels the uncinate connects (uncinate_cogat.dl of the engine tracts, its README and the assistant’s example of neurosynth_tracts) count the ends of the streamlines as the pairs count(x, e) of a streamline and which of its ends, rather than as the integers i == 2 * x + e.

  • The REPL in the browser starts with the engine Neurosynth + tracts (neurosynth_iit: Neurosynth with the tractogram and the bundles of the IIT atlas); Neurosynth and NeuroStore are in its select, and ?engine=neurosynth starts with Neurosynth alone. The bundle builds Neurosynth first all the same, whose text embeddings the tracts’ engine shares (DEFAULT_ENGINE of doc/make_repl_bundle.py).

  • The brain viewer of a result on the regions of atlases names the region under the mouse, and under the cursor, and draws the regions in the colours of their atlas (atlas colours), as a result of regions alone starts: Schaefer’s (from its label file), FreeSurfer’s for Destrieux (FreeSurferColorLUT.txt, fetched into the data directory) and Tian’s (the label file of its authors). The engine keeps the colours of its atlases (engine_registry.atlas_colours).

  • When the AI assistant can answer, with a key in the page or the one of the REPL’s configuration, the REPL in the browser starts with one of the engine’s example questions, at random, in the box of the assistant: selected, typing replaces it, and Ask asks it. WebRepl.assistant_ready tells the page.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --local serves the REPL page with NeuroLang in the Python of this machine rather than in the browser: the engine is built from the data directory with all of its analyses (lift('pain', i, j, k, l) answers in 9 s, where the browser’s engine leaves it out), Stop interrupts the query on the server, files are made there, and the AI assistant is the one configured on this machine unless the page gives a key. The page is the same; its worker, local_worker.js, sends what it is asked to the server.

  • neurolang-repl-serve --cert CERT.pem [--key KEY.pem] [--key-password-file FILE] serves over HTTPS (a key with a passphrase opened with that of the file, or asked in a terminal; a certificate that cannot serve says why), and --daemon install|reinstall|remove installs, replaces or removes a service of the user serving the REPL with the other options, at login: a unit of systemd’s user manager, or a launchd agent on macOS.

  • In NeurolangPDL, a rule whose body is only equalities to constants, p(l) :- (l == 'c'), is the fact p('c'): it raised an IndexError (#1076). The equalities to constants that the rewrite inlines are now only those of a variable with one of them and found elsewhere in atoms alone: (v == 'a') & (v == 'b') gave v = 'b', (v == 'b') & (w == v) dropped the constraint on w and (v == 'b') & (v != 'c') raised an UnboundVariablesError.

Performance:

  • Recursive rules: from the second iteration on, the semi-naive chase joined the whole relation again and re-evaluated the rules without a recursive atom. A rule with one recursive atom now has it bound to the tuples new since the previous iteration, and is skipped when there are none. A transitive closure of 2.56 M tuples takes 21 s and 1.8 GB instead of 49 s and 2.5 GB with pandas, and one of 10 M tuples 104 s instead of more than 120 s. Non-recursive queries are unchanged.

  • A probability over the choice of a study with a peak within a radius of each voxel, PROB[brain_mask(i, j, k), (euclidean(i, j, k, i1, j1, k1) < 3), peak_reported(i1, j1, k1, s), selected_study(s)], takes 13 s and half the memory, instead of 47 s and close to 10 GB, and now runs in a browser tab. The natural join with a relation unique on the only common column, as the probabilities of a choice are, is a look-up rather than a pandas merge; Python’s sum, max, min and len in an aggregation run as pandas reductions on columns of numbers without missing values; groups are not sorted; and the Euclidean join keeps no distances it is not asked for, numbers its pairs of rows with 32-bit integers and does not copy its result.

New features:

  • Confidence intervals by resampling the studies. nl.add_resampling("boot_studies", choice="selected_study"), or resamplings in an engine’s YAML, adds replicates of a probabilistic choice, the Bayesian, classical or Poisson bootstrap of its outcomes. RESAMPLE[boot_studies](pred(x)) = b is then pred(x), any rule over PROB of the choice including the measures computed from the probabilities, such as Bayes factors, on replicate b, and percentile(x, 2.5) and percentile(x, 97.5) over the replicates give the interval. Every query sees the same replicates, so that two measures are compared on the same b. It rewrites the rules into sums over the outcomes of the choice, weighted by each replicate, and needs no other solver; on Neurosynth, the interval of the Bayes factor of a term for a region takes 15 s. The neurosynth and neurostore engines have boot_studies, 500 and 200 replicates of selected_study, and the query assistant has examples and guidance for questions on how certain an answer is. v0.0.1 (Alpha)#

Initial alpha release of NeuroLang.

New features:

  • Datalog-based logic programming frontend (NeurolangDL)

  • Probabilistic extensions with independent probabilistic facts (NeurolangPDL)

  • Tuple-generating dependencies (TGDs) for open-world reasoning

  • Aggregation support (COUNT, MAX, SUM, custom callables)

  • Neuroimaging integration: nibabel image loading, Destrieux atlas, NeuroSynth

  • Example gallery: Destrieux gyri, sulcal queries, NeuroSynth implementation
