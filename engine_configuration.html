Engine Configuration (engines.yaml)#

Engines are declaratively configured in packages/neurolang_apps/neurolang_apps/utils/engines/engines.yaml and built by build_engine(). Each engine entry may specify the following keys, all of them optional:

  • extends — name (or list of names) of engines whose configuration is inherited, see Sharing configuration between engines

  • abstract — when true the entry only serves as a base for other engines: it is neither listed nor buildable

  • description — human-readable summary shown by --list-engines

  • requires_mni_mask — whether the engine needs an MNI brain mask (it is downloaded, optionally resampled with --resolution, and passed to the init phases)

  • use_base_symbols — when true (and an MNI mask is available), registers common neuroimaging symbols (agg_count, agg_create_region, agg_create_region_overlay, principal_direction, region_union)

  • builtins — known Python functions exposed to the Datalog solver; the available names are exp, log and startswith

  • python_init — dotted path of a Python module (not of a function) which must define init_engine(nl, mask, data_dir); it registers extensional data (predicates, regions, atlases) into the engine. It may be a list of modules, whose init_engine run in order; an engine extending others runs theirs first (the lists are concatenated, parent first)

  • solver_mixins — classes, as "module:Class" strings, put before the solver of the engine, e.g. to add Datalog commands .name(...) (the tracts engine, below)

  • python_init_options — keyword arguments given to every init_engine of python_init (one that has no use for the options of another takes them with **options). The neurosynth and neurostore engines take unknown_space, set once in coordinate_meta_analysis: the peaks whose space is neither MNI nor Talairach (Neurosynth’s UNKNOWN, 10.6% of its peaks; NeuroStore’s other or missing spaces) are taken as MNI (mni, the default, as Neurosynth and NiMARE do), converted as Talairach (tal) or left out (drop); only the Talairach peaks are converted to MNI otherwise

  • downloads — list of {url, dest, extract, name, md5} entries fetched into the data directory (extract: true uncompresses an archive, a list of member names extracts only those members; name is the file’s name when the URL has none, md5 its checksum)

  • tractogram — {path, subsample, step, seed}, for an engine whose datalog_init loads the whole-brain tractogram file path (relative to the data directory; usually one of its downloads) with .load_tractogram(id, path): how the bundle of the browser’s REPL (doc/make_repl_bundle.py) reduces it to ship it. It replaces the file in the data it ships by its subsample, at the same path and in the same format (.tck or .trk), subsample streamlines drawn at random with the seed seed (default 0), each thinned to every step-th point (default 1) and its last, so that the engine’s program is the same there. The engine itself loads the file whole, wherever it runs; the entry is read by the bundle only (neurosynth_iit: 15,000 of the 37,980 streamlines of the IIT Human Brain Atlas v3’s IIT3mean.tck, step: 3)

  • templates — MNI templates fetched with nilearn or TemplateFlow, optionally registered as a predicate(i, j, k) of non-zero voxels

  • atlases — declarative atlas loading (destrieux, schaefer, tian, difumo) as region predicates name(label, region). A deterministic atlas is a Atlas, fetched and read as .load_atlas(name, kind="destrieux") ("schaefer", "tian") reads it, its label names normalised (- and _ are spaces), and recorded in the engine’s atlases by its key, where .index_tracts of the tracts engine finds it (the probabilistic DiFuMo is thresholded into regions). A deterministic atlas with activated: {predicate_name, radius_mm, peaks} also registers predicate_name(label, study): the studies with a peak within radius_mm (by default the engine’s kernel_radius_mm, else 3) of a voxel of the region, the peaks being the millimetre coordinates of the relation peaks (x, y, z, study, peak_mni by default). The distances are measured in millimetres through the atlas’s affine, so its grid need not be the template’s, and the pairs are kept in the data directory once found (see Starting an engine without finding its tables again). With points: {predicate_name} a deterministic atlas also registers predicate_name(x, y, z, label): the centre of each voxel of each region, in millimetres through the atlas’s affine, a stored relation, so that a rule bounding the distance of these points to those of another stored relation, EUCLIDEAN(x, y, z, x2, y2, z2) <= r, finds the pairs with a k-d tree instead of comparing every pair; the engines extending mni_atlases have schaefer_points, destrieux_points and tian_points (236,000 rows, 13 MB). tian is the Melbourne subcortex atlas (Tian et al., 2020, Nat Neurosci, doi:10.1038/s41593-020-00711-6, whose licence asks for this citation), 3T, scale 1 to 4 (16, 32, 50 or 54 regions), downloaded from the authors’ repository

  • euclidean_join — settings of the Euclidean join that finds the pairs of points of a EUCLIDEAN(...) < r bound that differ from the profile in use (kdtree_chunk_pairs: 500000): the thresholds, the sizes of the chunks and the other values of neurolang/utils/euclidean_join.yaml, whose profiles (default, and memory, that of the interactive REPL) hold the others; see Memory and time of Euclidean distance bounds. The mappings of an engine and of the engines it extends are merged

  • datalog_init — inline Datalog rules that are loaded at engine startup, run from the engine’s data directory: the paths of its commands (.load_tractogram, .load_atlas…) are relative to it, where downloads puts the files. {kernel_radius_voxels} in them is kernel_radius_mm in voxels of the engine’s grid

  • kernel_radius_mm — the radius, in millimetres, of the sphere around a reported peak that a study activates, the same at every resolution: the bound of activated ({kernel_radius_voxels} in datalog_init, 3 voxels on the 2 mm grid, 6 on the 1 mm one), the radius_mm of the activated atlases that do not set theirs, and the number written for {kernel_radius_mm} in the texts of the engine (descriptions, assistant_guidance, examples). The coordinate meta-analysis engines take 6 mm, the radius of the spheres of Neurosynth’s activation maps (its software’s default, r=6; see Neurosynth’s Association and Uniformity Tests as Probability Ratios); ALE instead blurs each peak with a Gaussian whose width depends on the number of subjects of the study (Eickhoff et al., 2009)

  • relations — CSV/TSV files (local paths or URLs) loaded as extensional predicates

  • probabilistic_choice — uniform probabilistic choices over an existing predicate, declared as YAML mappings (source and an optional description)

  • resamplings — replicates of a probabilistic choice for confidence intervals, name: {choice: selected_study, n: 500, scheme: bayesian, seed: 0}, queried with RESAMPLE[name](pred(x)) = b (see the tutorial Datalog: A Rule-Based Query Language for NeuroLang, section 10.7)

  • ontologies — list of OWL/RDF ontologies to load: url (a URL or a local path) or urls (several files loaded together), and the format, individuals and names options of load_ontology()

  • text_embeddings — semantic search over document texts, registered as a similarity function (see Text embeddings below)

  • predicates — schema metadata (arity, column names, description) used by --list-predicates, the interactive REPL and the web UI; see Predicate arguments and sorts below

  • hidden_predicates — predicates the interactive REPL does not offer in SQUALL, because their argument order breaks sentences (they remain usable when typed)

  • dimension_type_predicates — list of predicate names that SQUALL queries may use as dimension-type annotations; they are stripped from the rules before solving (defaults to probability and value)

  • assistant_guidance — how the LLM query assistant (.ask, neurolang-query --ask) is to answer questions on the engine: added to its system prompt. The coordinate meta-analysis engines ask for probabilistic answers over selected_study (counts for “how many”), activation as a peak within kernel_radius_mm of a point in MNI millimetres, the Destrieux atlas for the classical anatomy of the cortex and the Schaefer and Tian atlases for the functional anatomy

  • examples — example queries, as lists under datalog and squall; the interactive REPL suggests the first one of its language at startup and lists them all in .help (by default it suggests obtaining the first declared predicate)

  • questions — questions in English that the REPL in the browser lists first, a click putting one in the box of the query assistant; each is one of the questions of assistant_examples, of which the tests check the queries. The query examples are folded under them

  • assistant_examples — the bank of questions with their datalog or squall queries from which the LLM query assistant takes its few-shot examples (see LLM Query Assistant)

The long entries, examples, assistant_examples and assistant_guidance, are kept out of engines.yaml, in files of the engine’s directory next to it (see Files of an engine).

The init phases run in the order described in Concepts: builtins, base symbols, Python init, downloads, templates, atlases, Datalog init, relations, text embeddings, probabilistic choices and ontologies.

Example — the neurosynth engine (abridged):

engines:
  neurosynth:
    description: >
      Neurosynth database — forward and reverse inference over
      reported activation peaks, TF-IDF term frequencies, and a
      probabilistic choice over studies.
    requires_mni_mask: true
    use_base_symbols: true
    builtins: [exp, log, startswith]
    python_init: "neurolang.utils.engines.neurosynth.init"
    atlases:
      schaefer:
        predicate_name: schaefer
        n_rois: 100
        resolution_mm: 2
        yeo_networks: 17
    datalog_init: |
      study_with_peaks(S) :- peak_reported(I, J, K, S)
      mentions(s, t) :- term_in_study_tfidf(s, t, v), (v > 0.03)
      weighted_mention(s, t, w) :- term_in_study_tfidf(s, t, w)
    predicates:
      peak_reported:
        arity: 4
        columns: [i, j, k, study_id]
        description: "Reported activation peaks in voxel coordinates"
      study:
        arity: 1
        columns: [study_id]
        description: "All study identifiers"
      term_in_study_tfidf:
        arity: 3
        columns: [study_id, term, tfidf]
        description: "Term frequency–inverse document frequency values"

In this engine term_in_study_tfidf has columns (study_id, term, tfidf), and the derived mentions(s, t) relation keeps the same order: (study_id, term). weighted_mention(study_id, term, tfidf) holds the same rows as term_in_study_tfidf under a name that reads better in SQUALL, where an n-ary relation is a noun followed by a tuple of variables, in the order of its columns:

obtain every Weighted_mention (?s; ?t; ?w)
    such that ?t is 'pain' and ?w is greater than 0.1.

SQUALL verbs relate two arguments (a Study mentions a Term), so a relation of three columns cannot be a verb. The selected_study uniform choice over studies is registered by the Python init module.

Predicate arguments and sorts#

A predicate may declare its arguments: the entities it relates, in column order, each of a sort spanning one or more columns of a type. The arity of a predicate, in the sense of the entities it relates, is the number of its arguments, not of its columns: a voxel (i, j, k) is one argument, so reports(study_id, i, j, k) relates a study and a voxel:

reports:
  description: "Studies reporting an activation peak at a voxel"
  arguments:
    - {sort: study, columns: [study_id], type: int}
    - {sort: voxel, columns: [i, j, k], type: int}

columns and arity (the number of columns) are then derived from the arguments, and entities holds the number of arguments; declared columns or arity must agree with them. A sort spans the same columns everywhere. probability: true marks a probabilistic symbol, whose probability column is not an argument. get_signatures() returns the arguments of each predicate.

A sort of points may declare its space: space: voxel marks the indices (i, j, k) of a grid, and space: mm coordinates (x, y, z) in millimetres in the space of the engine’s template, the MNI image of an engine with requires_mni_mask (nl.template on the engine build_engine() returns). The grid of a sort of voxels is that of the template unless the sort names another with grid: that of an atlas of the engine, by its key under atlases. The Schaefer atlas of neurosynth has its own 2 mm grid, which labels and schaefer_voxels index:

atlases:
  schaefer: {n_rois: 100, resolution_mm: 2, yeo_networks: 17}
sorts:
  voxel: {columns: [i, j, k], type: int, space: voxel}
  mni: {columns: [x, y, z], type: float, space: mm}
  atlas_voxel:
    columns: [i, j, k]
    type: int
    space: voxel
    grid: schaefer

A grid is template or the key of an atlas of the engine, and only a sort of space: voxel names one; the configuration is rejected otherwise. The engine holds the image of each grid in nl.grids, by name: the template’s, as template, and each atlas’s, registered when the atlas is loaded.

The REPL’s .save out.nii.gz (and .view) writes a result on a grid, with its shape and affine. Each column of the result has the sort of the argument of the predicate its variable is in, in the last query: ans(x, y, z) :- voxel(x, y, z) holds voxel indices of the template grid, whatever the names of its columns, ans(l, i, j, k) :- labels(l, i, j, k) indices of the Schaefer atlas grid. With a template, each atlas is resampled to it (nearest neighbour) when it is loaded, so the voxel (i, j, k) of an atlas is that of voxel and of the peaks. The sorts follow from the predicates declaring their arguments, then from the rules defined from them, in Datalog as in SQUALL (obtain every Voxel in 3D). The columns of a space: voxel sort are written as they are on its grid, those of an mm sort converted to the nearest voxel of the template. A column of unknown sort, such as one of a relation loaded with .load, is of the sort it is named as. --voxel I J K and --mm X Y Z give the columns, on the template’s grid (see .help save).

A sort of region labels may name, with atlas, the atlas whose regions it labels, by its key under atlases; it has one column and no space:

destrieux_label:
  columns: [label]
  type: str
  atlas: destrieux

A result with a column of such a sort is then a result on the regions of the atlas: .save out.nii.gz and .view paint each region, on the atlas’s grid, with the value of its row (ans(l, p) :- PROB[...]), and .view shows a result with two such columns, pairs of regions and a value, as a network over the brain, a node at the centre of each region and an edge per pair (see .help view). The values labelling each region in the image of the atlas, by name, are those of the atlas the engine records under that key, nl.atlases (see atlas_label_values()).

The interactive REPL completes SQUALL from the sorts: after every it offers the nouns, capitalised; after every Study that the verbs whose first entity is a study (mentions, reports); after every Term that the object clause a Study mentions; after a noun of several columns in 3D and the tuple (?i; ?j; ?k), and with Probability after a relation defined with inferred probability. In Datalog, the columns of an entity are completed together (i, j, k). The inverse verb form ~verb is not offered, as SQUALL does not invert its arguments in a relative clause; every Term that a Study mentions reads the same.

A note on the datalog_init syntax: the text Datalog parser does not distinguish variables from predicate names by case, so S and s are both variables, and the final . of a rule is optional. Comparisons must be written between parentheses, as in (v > 0.03). See Datalog: A Rule-Based Query Language for NeuroLang for the full syntax.

Text embeddings#

A text_embeddings entry embeds the title and abstract of every document of a relation with SPECTER2 and registers a function function(query, doc) returning the cosine similarity between a free-text query and a document, nan for an id that is not in the relation (see Semantic Search Over Study Abstracts):

text_embeddings:
  study_abstracts:
    ids_from: study                 # first column holds the document ids
    source: pubmed                  # ids are PubMed ids
    model: allenai/specter2_base
    document_adapter: allenai/specter2
    query_adapter: allenai/specter2_adhoc_query
    function: query_similarity
    description: "Similarity between a query and a study abstract"

source is pubmed (titles and abstracts fetched from the NCBI E-utilities), a table of texts, local or remote:

source:
  file: https://example.org/documents.tsv
  id_column: id
  text_columns: [title, abstract]   # first column is the title

or texts the engine already holds as predicates p(id, text), which is how the meta-analysis engines embed the titles and abstracts of their studies, study_title and study_abstract (see The publications of the studies):

source:
  predicate: study_abstract
  title_predicate: study_title      # optional

model, document_adapter and query_adapter default to the values above. The engine build only reads the ids. Texts are fetched and embedded on the first query that uses the function, and cached under {data_dir}/{engine}/embeddings/. An engine whose entry is that of an engine it extends shares its caches: {engine} is the most basic non-abstract engine of its extends chain with the same resolved entry (neurosynth for neurosynth_iit, neurostore for neurostore_iit; see engine_registry.embeddings_owner). The engine keeps embeddings in its own directory when the shared ones are of other documents (its study set differs), or when only its own directory has them. The embeddings can be computed ahead of time with python -m neurolang.utils.text_embeddings --engine neurosynth --data-dir neurolang_data.

Starting an engine without finding its tables again#

Most of the build of the neurosynth engine, 58 s natively and 112 s in a browser, finds what never changes for the same data: the studies activating the regions of the atlases (48 s natively), and, from nilearn, the MNI mask resampled to the grid of the engine, the voxels of the brain and of the cortex on it, and where the atlases are, for which nilearn and scikit-learn are imported, which takes more than a second and several in a browser. build_engine() keeps all of them in DATA_DIR/ENGINE/engine_cache, in files named by their kind and by what they depend on, and reads them when they are there. Then nilearn is not imported at all, the build takes 6 s natively and 8 s in a browser, and the page starts in 23 s instead of 127 s. A change of the atlas, the peaks, the radius or the resolution makes other files; a file that is missing, damaged or cannot be written means only that the table is found again. The MNI mask is named by the resolution alone, so remove the files (--clear) if the template changes.

The files hold arrays, an image and text, nothing of Python or of a version of numpy or pandas, so the terminal REPL and the REPL in the browser read the same ones, whichever implementation of Python wrote them. The engine itself cannot be kept: its expressions cannot be pickled, and a snapshot of the memory of Pyodide is 780 MB (270 MB compressed).

python -m neurolang_apps.utils.engine_cache ENGINE -d DATA_DIR -r MM builds the engine at the resolution it will be used with, so that the tables are kept; --list lists them and --clear removes them; neurolang-query --no-engine-cache builds the engine without reading or writing them. The build of the browser’s bundle (doc/make_repl_bundle.py, run by the documentation workflow) keeps them in the data zip, 3.6 MB, builds the engine from them and without them to check that the trimmed data suffices for both, and stops if it kept none.

Sharing configuration between engines#

Settings common to several engines live in an abstract engine that the concrete ones pull in with extends. The built-in neurosynth and neurostore engines both extend coordinate_meta_analysis, which provides the MNI mask, the base symbols, the sorts, the predicates and their Datalog rules, and the text embeddings; it extends mni_atlases, the atlases Destrieux, Schaefer and Tian with the sorts, predicates and rules of their labels and regions, and adds to each atlas the studies that activate its regions (activated), which need the peaks. The tracts engine extends mni_atlases too, and so has the same atlases, loaded the same way. Each engine adds its python_init and what is specific to its database:

engines:
  mni_atlases:
    abstract: true
    atlases:
      destrieux:
        predicate_name: destrieux
        points: {predicate_name: destrieux_points}
      tian:
        predicate_name: tian
        scale: 2
        points: {predicate_name: tian_points}

  coordinate_meta_analysis:
    abstract: true
    extends: mni_atlases
    atlases:      # merged into those of mni_atlases, atlas by atlas
      destrieux:
        activated: {predicate_name: destrieux_activated}
      tian:
        activated: {predicate_name: tian_activated}
    kernel_radius_mm: 6
    requires_mni_mask: true
    use_base_symbols: true
    datalog_init: |
      study_with_peaks(s) :- peak_reported(i, j, k, s).
    text_embeddings:
      study_abstracts: {ids_from: study, source: pubmed,
                        function: query_similarity}

  neurostore:
    extends: coordinate_meta_analysis
    python_init: "neurolang.utils.engines.neurostore.init"
    text_embeddings:
      study_abstracts:
        source: {predicate: study_abstract}   # replaces ``pubmed``

With several bases (extends: [a, b]) later ones override earlier ones, and the engine’s own entries override all of them. Merging works per key:

  • datalog_init texts are concatenated, inherited rules first;

  • builtins, downloads, ontologies, dimension_type_predicates, assistant_examples, hidden_predicates, solver_mixins and python_init (a single module is a list of one) are concatenated without duplicates;

  • mappings (atlases, templates, relations, sorts, predicates, text_embeddings, …) are merged recursively by OmegaConf, so an engine can change one parameter of an inherited entry; setting an entry to null removes the inherited one (a list cannot replace a mapping, nor a mapping a list);

  • every other value is replaced (description, requires_mni_mask, …).

A base reached by two paths (extends: [a, b] where both a and b extend c) is merged once, where it is first reached: its rules are not defined twice. Unknown bases and cyclic extends chains raise ValueError.

Files of an engine#

Every file <engine>/*.yaml in the directory of the built-in engines.yaml is merged into the entry of its engine, in the order of the file names, with OmegaConf (OmegaConf.merge, which merges mappings and replaces lists: a key is best set in one file only). The built-in engines keep there

  • <engine>/examples.yaml: the queries the REPL shows (examples) and the questions of the query assistant (assistant_examples);

  • <engine>/guidance.yaml: the assistant_guidance.

coordinate_meta_analysis/ holds the examples and the guidance shared by neurosynth and neurostore, and neurostore/examples.yaml adds the questions on NeuroStore’s own predicates: an engine inherits assistant_examples as it inherits builtins, those of its bases first. The OmegaConf interpolation ${engines.<engine>.<key>} can refer to any value of the composed configuration. An engines file given to the functions of engine_registry as yaml_path is read alone.

The neurostore engine#

neurostore loads the NeuroStore database (the one behind Neurosynth Compose, which NiMARE fetches with fetch_neurostore). It downloads a release archive from neurostore.org, verifies its checksum and caches it under <data_dir>/neurostore/<version>; NEUROLANG_NEUROSTORE_RELEASE selects the release (latest, nightly or e.g. 2026-10). It has all the predicates of neurosynth, with the same names and columns. Study ids are NeuroStore’s alphanumeric strings. NeuroStore ships no term table, so term_in_study_tfidf is computed from the study abstracts with scikit-learn (words and sequences of two or three, so that terms such as working memory exist; English stop words, terms in at least 30 studies, values above 1e-3) and is not comparable with Neurosynth’s curated-vocabulary values. Peaks in spaces other than MNI or Talairach are dropped. It adds study_title(study_id, title), study_abstract(study_id, abstract), and study_concept(study_id, concept) / study_domain(study_id, domain), the cognitive concepts and domains that an LLM extracted from the tasks of each study.

Like neurosynth, it defines the lift, relative_risk, uniformity and ratios of a term (Neurosynth’s Association and Uniformity Tests as Probability Ratios). With SPECTER2 over its abstracts it also defines them for a free text, uniformity_llm('a description', i, j, k, u) and the like.

Its SPECTER2 query_similarity(query, study) embeds those titles and abstracts; precompute the embeddings with python -m neurolang.utils.text_embeddings --engine neurostore --data-dir neurolang_data.

The publications of the studies#

neurosynth and neurostore, and the engines extending them, have the title, abstract, year and journal of each study: study_title(study_id, title), study_abstract(study_id, abstract), study_year(study_id, year) and study_journal(study_id, journal), so that a query, and the query assistant, restricts the studies to years or journals and conditions on them as on a term:

recent(s) :- study_year(s, y), (y >= 2010), (y <= 2015)
active(l, s) :- destrieux_activated(l, s), selected_study(s)
cond(s) :- recent(s), mentions(s, 'pain'), selected_study(s)
ans(l, p) :- PROB[active(l, s) // cond(s)] = p

Neurosynth’s metadata holds the titles, years and journals; the abstracts are PubMed’s, asked for at the first build of the engine (about 70 requests of two hundred studies, four to five minutes; less with an NCBI_API_KEY) and kept in <data_dir>/neurosynth/pubmed_abstracts.json. A NeuroStore release holds the titles, abstracts and journals but not the years, PubMed ids and DOIs: they are those NeuroStore lists for its studies, about twenty requests of ten thousand studies (700 MB, a few minutes) at the first build, kept in <data_dir>/neurostore/<version>-studies.parquet. Both also have the identifiers of each study, study_pmid(study_id, pmid), its PubMed id as an integer, and study_doi(study_id, doi), its DOI in lower case without https://doi.org/, the same in every engine: a Neurosynth study’s id is its PubMed id, and NeuroStore lists those of its studies (97% have one, 99% a DOI; 14,358 of the 14,371 Neurosynth studies are in NeuroStore’s 2026-11 release). The PubMed ids match the studies of the two databases: the studies of a NeuroStore query saved with .save and loaded in Neurosynth, joined on study_pmid. Match on them rather than on the DOIs, which the two databases give differently for 1.8% of their common studies (one of them wrong: Neurosynth gives two studies the same DOI). The next builds ask for nothing; without the network, the studies not kept have no abstract or year, with a warning, until the next build. The journals are named as each database names them (Neuron, but The Journal of neuroscience : the official journal of the Society for Neuroscience in Neurosynth). In the browser the tables are those of the page’s data.

The Cognitive Atlas#

Both neurosynth and neurostore load the Cognitive Atlas ontology with ontologies (see Querying the Cognitive Atlas Ontology), next to the cognitive_task relation: its classes, labels, definitions and axioms are facts and constraints that queries use with the engine’s data, in the open-world manner of NeuroLang. Because ontologies is loaded before datalog_init, the rules of an engine can use the ontology’s predicates, as cogat_subclass_of and cogat_measures do.

The tracts engine#

tracts is tractography, neurolang_apps.utils.engines.tracts (the extra tracts of neurolang-apps; without it the engine fails to build, with a message that says so). It extends mni_atlases: its atlases are those of neurosynth (Destrieux, Schaefer, Tian, about 270 KB downloaded on the first build), without the studies that activate them and without the MNI mask. A program loads its tractograms with the commands .load_tractogram and .index_tracts, which its solver_mixins entry, neurolang_apps.utils.engines.tracts.frontend:TractsCommandsMixin, adds to the solver; .index_tracts(t, "destrieux") indexes a tractogram in MNI152 by an atlas of the engine, whose label names are those of its relation: label("destrieux", l, name, h) joins destrieux(name, region). An atlas of its own is loaded as any engine loads one, .load_atlas("fs", "wmparc.mgz", "FreeSurferColorLUT.txt", kind="freesurfer", space="MNI152"), and indexed by its name; .index_tracts gives its labels the terms of the vocabulary by the term mapping of its kind (FreeSurfer, Destrieux, Tian) or by term_mapping=.... It asks where the streamlines go in the vocabulary of tractography, Datalog rules that its python_init (neurolang_apps.utils.engines.tracts.frontend) gives the engine: traverses(x, term, h), endpoint_in(x, e, term, h), anterior_of(x, term, h) and the like (the engine’s DESIGN.md, section 16). The same init_engine gives it the hooks with which .view draws a result of streamline ids x on the label image of the atlas. The ends of the streamlines are also points in millimetres, endpoint_point(x, e, px, py, pz), stored as the points of the atlases are, so that the parcels near the ends are found with a tree:

near(x, e, n) :- endpoint_point(x, e, ex, ey, ez),
    schaefer_points(mx, my, mz, n),
    (EUCLIDEAN(ex, ey, ez, mx, my, mz) <= 3)

The uncinate fasciculus of tract_querier is the engine’s examples/uncinate.dl:

neurolang-query --engine tracts --file uncinate.dl
neurolang-query --engine tracts -i --file uncinate.dl   # then .view

The neurosynth_tracts engine#

neurosynth_tracts is both, extends: [neurosynth, meta_analysis_tracts], the abstract engine meta_analysis_tracts extending coordinate_meta_analysis and tracts: the studies, terms, peaks and Cognitive Atlas of neurosynth, its atlases with the studies that activate their regions (schaefer_activated(label, study)…) and the centres of their voxels in millimetres (schaefer_points(x, y, z, label)…), and the commands, vocabulary and .view of tracts. Their common base mni_atlases is merged once, and both python_init run, neurosynth’s then neurolang_apps.utils.engines.tracts.frontend. It needs the extra tracts, and embeddings for similar_study (SPECTER2; without it a query that uses it fails with a message saying what to install). The ends of the streamlines of a bundle, endpoint_point(x, e, x, y, z), join the points of the parcels within a distance, and the parcels the studies that activate them: The engine’s examples/uncinate_cogat.dl asks which Cognitive Atlas concepts the studies of the parcels the uncinate fasciculus connects are about:

neurolang-query --engine neurosynth_tracts --file uncinate_cogat.dl

neurosynth_iit adds the abstract engine iit_tractogram: the downloads and the commands that load the whole-brain tractogram of the IIT Human Brain Atlas v3, index it by its wmparc and import tract_querier’s bundles. neurostore_tracts (extends: [neurostore, meta_analysis_tracts]) and neurostore_iit (extends: [neurostore_tracts, iit_tractogram]) are the same over the studies of neurostore, with the same questions of the query assistant.

To add a new engine, append an entry to engines.yaml and (if needed) provide a python_init module whose init_engine function populates the engine’s symbol table. The neurolang-query CLI and the web server will automatically pick up the new engine.
